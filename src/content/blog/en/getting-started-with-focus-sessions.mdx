You sit down to write one report. Ninety minutes later you've answered six emails and read two Slack threads. The report is still a blank page. Nothing went wrong, exactly. The day just had no shape.

A focus session gives it one. It is a timed block where you promise yourself one piece of work, and something else keeps the clock. This post shows how to run your first sessions, what to look at afterwards, and a simple plan for your first week.

## What a focus session is (and what it isn't)

A focus session is a block of time with a start, an end and a single task. It turns "I should focus" into "I'm focusing on this, until then."

A session doesn't lock you out of anything. FocusNow doesn't block sites or send alerts when you drift. It keeps the time and an honest record of the block. What you do with that record is up to you.

## Pomodoro or Deep Work: pick by the task

The Focus page, where FocusNow opens, has four timer modes:

- **Pomodoro**: 25 minutes by default. Good for tasks you keep putting off, fuzzy tasks, or days when starting feels hard.
- **Deep Work**: 90 minutes by default. Good for work that needs your whole brain: writing, design, code, studying for an exam.
- **Short Break** and **Long Break**: 5 and 15 minutes by default. Breaks run on the timer only and are never recorded.

Every length can be changed, from 1 to 180 minutes, in **Settings → Focus & Sessions**. If 90 minutes feels like too much, start with 50. The right length is the one you'll actually start.

## Your first session in six steps

1. Open FocusNow. It starts on the **Focus** page. You can switch modes with the keys 1 to 4.
2. Pick a mode. For a first try, Pomodoro is the safe choice.
3. Choose one task from the task list under the timer. Give it a rough time estimate if you like.
4. Press **Start**. If you want the timer visible over other windows, turn on the floating session widget in Settings → Focus & Sessions.
5. Work on that one task. If the phone rings, pause and resume. The widget has its own pause button.
6. When time is up, or when you're done early, the session is saved with its real length. Press **End session** to stop early. A short summary takes the place of the timer.

## What a finished session tells you

Switch the Focus page from **Timer** to **History**. Sessions are grouped by day. Each row shows the type, when it started and how much of the target you reached, such as "61m of 90m". Click a row to see every app and site you used during that block.

That's the whole report. There's no grade for a single session. You see how much of the target you completed, and where the minutes went.

Your [Focus Score](/guide/focus-score) is a separate, daily number. It comes from all your tracked activity in the day (or only your working hours, if you set them), not just from sessions. Sessions don't add bonus points. But time you spend focused inside a session shows up there, like any other focused time.

## What one morning can look like

Here's an example of a first real try. Say you're a freelance designer with a client brief due on Friday.

At 9:00 you start a Pomodoro on the task you've been avoiding: writing the brief's first draft. You stay in your text editor, and the session ends at 25 of 25 minutes. You take a short break.

At 9:35 you start a Deep Work block on logo ideas. At minute 61 a client calls, and you end the session. History shows "61m of 90m". When you open it, you see your design tool for most of the block, but also twelve minutes in a chat app in the middle.

Nothing failed here. The task you dreaded took one Pomodoro. And chat cut into your best block, so next time you close it first.

## Common mistakes in the first week

- **Starting with 90 minutes every time.** Long blocks are a skill. Build up from Pomodoros.
- **Picking a vague task.** "Work on the website" is a project. "Write the About page text" is a task you can finish.
- **Calling a short session a failure.** A session you ended at minute 14 is still recorded, with its real length. It tells you something about your day.
- **Running sessions all day.** Email and meetings are work too. Save sessions for the work that needs them.
- **Never opening History.** The timer helps you start. The record helps you improve.
- **Leaving categories that don't fit you.** YouTube is a distraction for an accountant and research for a video editor. In **Activities**, use the ⋯ menu on any app or site to change its category to Focus, Neutral, Personal or Distraction.

## A simple plan for your first week

1. **Days 1–2:** run one Pomodoro each morning, before you open email.
2. **Days 3–4:** run two or three Pomodoros in the morning. Look at History at lunch.
3. **Day 5:** try one Deep Work block. Put it in the hour where your focus looks strongest. The **Focus score by hour** chart on the Productivity page shows this once you have a few days of data.
4. **End of the week:** open Productivity, choose This week, and look at your focus time and average Focus Score. Then plan next week's deep blocks around your best hours.

If your laptop is also your evening sofa, set [working hours](/guide/working-hours) so your score only covers the workday.

## Quick answers

### Does FocusNow block distracting sites during a session?

No. FocusNow doesn't block apps or sites, and it doesn't send alerts. It keeps the time and shows you what happened, so you can decide.

### Is tracking only on during sessions?

No. While FocusNow is running, it records the app and window in front of you all day. Sessions mark the blocks you planned. Quitting the app stops tracking.
